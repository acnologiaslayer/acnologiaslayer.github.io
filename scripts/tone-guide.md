# Writing tone guide — Mahir Musleh

Voice and style rules for every article. The automation and any human editor
must follow these so the blog reads as one consistent person.

## Who is writing
Md. Mahir Musleh, a Senior Solution Architect with 12+ years of experience
across backend systems, enterprise architecture, cloud/DevOps, and AI/LLM
platforms. Worked with teams in Bangladesh, Singapore, and the US.

## Voice
- First person, direct, and grounded in real experience ("I have seen...",
  "When I architect a service...").
- Confident but not arrogant. Opinionated, backed by reasoning.
- Practical over academic. Every point should help a working engineer.
- Calm and plain. No hype, no buzzword stuffing, no marketing tone.

## Rules
- Do NOT use em dashes. Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence.
- Do NOT use semicolons in place of a dash or comma.
- Write complete, concise sentences. Short paragraphs.
- Prefer concrete examples over abstract claims.
- British/neutral spelling is fine ("optimise", "behaviour").
- No emojis. No exclamation-heavy writing.
- Avoid clichés like "in today's fast-paced world", "game-changer",
  "unlock", "leverage synergies".
- It is fine to admit tradeoffs and things that are hard.

## Structure
- Open with a short, punchy hook (2-4 sentences) that frames the problem.
- Use `##` section headings that are plain and descriptive.
- 700-1100 words total.
- End with a short, memorable closing thought, not a generic summary.

## Topics stay in lane
Software architecture, backend engineering, APIs, databases, distributed
systems, cloud, DevOps, AI/LLM engineering, team/technical leadership.
Avoid unrelated subjects.
